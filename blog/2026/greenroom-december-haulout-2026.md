Greenroom is booked onto the hardstand from **Friday 11 December to Wednesday 23 December 2026**. That's 13 calendar days to sort out some overdue hull work, remove the old corroded underwater fittings, fit new drains, and repaint the hull.

This page is the working plan and progress tracker. I'll update the checklists, decisions, products and daily notes as the job progresses.

**Boat:** Greenroom — 1976 Space Sailer 22  
**Haul-out:** Friday 11 December 2026  
**Fixed relaunch deadline:** Wednesday 23 December 2026  
**Status:** Planning  
**Priority:** Sound, watertight repairs first; good durable coatings second; cosmetic perfection last.

> **Non-negotiable booking:** The boat must leave the hardstand on **Wednesday 23 December**. Work scope is flexible; the lift-out date isn't. The default is to relaunch a safe, watertight boat, even if antifoul or topsides paint has to wait until an early-January haul-out. **Never launch with unsafe repairs, open hull penetrations, exposed underwater laminate requiring sealing, or coatings that are not approved/cured for immersion.** If a safe relaunch becomes impossible, arrange an alternative way off the hardstand (e.g. yard-approved relocation or transport) rather than rushing repairs.

## Deadline, priorities and stop-work rules

**The aim is a safe relaunch on 23 December, not completing every cosmetic job.** An early-January haul-out is the fallback for deferred paint and fairing. The following order governs the work when the schedule slips:

| Level | Requirement | December rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P1 — Seaworthy** | Sound laminate and properly sealed old holes; no unsafe open penetrations; safe drains; no serious keel defects; adequate cure before immersion | Mandatory. Protect time for this before any other job. |
| **P2 — Protected** | All newly exposed *underwater* laminate sealed using an immersion-suitable system; bare keel substrate protected as required | Mandatory wherever work exposes material that cannot safely remain immersed; can avoid opening sound existing coating. |
| **P3 — Finish** | Smooth fairing, full cosmetic topsides finish, complete antifoul renewal and optional coating upgrades | Defer to January as needed. Fresh antifoul is not automatically essential for a short interval if existing protection remains adequate and any new coating is suitable for immersion. |

**Important:** Don't partially strip the underwater hull unless there is enough time to restore the necessary immersion-resistant coating protection. A full epoxy *barrier* programme may not be safely splittable across two haul-outs without manufacturer guidance. If work slips, leave sound original underwater coatings intact wherever possible.

**Rule for starting any new job:** Before sanding through a sound coating, drilling a hole or opening a blister, ask: *Can I fully repair, seal, cure and test this by 22 December?* If not, photograph it and add it to January's list unless it is a genuine safety-critical defect. Don't deliberately leave a newly exposed underwater surface unprotected for immersion.

## Known arrangements and design choices

### Old through-hulls

The existing cockpit drains exit **just below the waterline** through old, badly corroded bronze fittings. There are **three old hull openings in total** to be removed and glassed over. Confirm which opening serves which system when the boat is lifted; don't assume all three are cockpit drains.

Each old penetration needs a proper structural laminate repair, not just a plug of putty. The laminate schedule must be based on the actual hole size, hull thickness, glass type and access. Taper/scarf the surrounding sound laminate, clean and dry it, apply appropriate fibreglass reinforcement with compatible epoxy, cure and fair. Inspect both sides where accessible and document the layup.

### New cockpit drains

The cockpit floor is roughly **300 mm above the upright waterline**. The two replacement **25 mm TruDesign outlets** are planned through the hull sides, served by short hoses running downhill through the aft locker. The aft locker is described as sealed off from the cabin, although a flooded locker could still affect trim and stability.

Crossed drain hoses sound attractive but may not maintain enough fall when heeled; the current preference is **short, direct and continuously descending runs**. The outlet positions may become submerged when racing at heel.

**Important unresolved issue:** An outlet that is above the upright waterline but below the *heeled* waterline must not automatically be treated as a simple above-waterline fitting. Confirm the TruDesign fitting model and its permitted installation, the applicable sailing/cockpit-drain requirements, and whether an isolating valve or a different approved arrangement is needed **before drilling**. A watertight locker reduces one flooding path but does not establish that a valve-less installation is approved or safe. Also verify that the locker really is watertight, how it drains, and what happens if it floods.

Two 25 mm drains have not yet been verified as adequate for cockpit volume and intended use. Confirm actual **internal bore** and drainage capacity; fitting nominal size does not necessarily equal hose bore.

### New anchor-well drain

The existing anchor well has a small blocked drain that exits below the waterline. A new **25 mm outlet above the upright waterline** is planned, subject to workable fall, the heeled-waterline assessment, and a suitable hose or direct-drain route. Confirm that the well is isolated from the accommodation and that the outlet cannot readily backflood the boat. Provide access for cleaning mud and weed out of the drain.

## Working methods and quality checkpoints

### 1. Lift, wash and inspection

Pressure wash promptly, before growth dries hard. Inspect the underwater hull after it is clean and dry enough to see the coating and laminate. Photograph osmosis blisters, cracking, previous repairs, keel pitting and the hull/keel joint. Mark each repair area.

If osmosis appears widespread, stop and reassess. Local blisters can only be repaired on this schedule if affected material can be properly removed, cleaned and dried to a suitable condition. Don't trap moisture beneath an epoxy barrier just to meet the launch date. Where moisture readings or laminate condition are uncertain, get competent inspection advice.

### 2. Remove and glass over old through-hulls

Remove the old bronze fittings, seacocks/adaptors and redundant hoses. Verify the three openings and check for wet, damaged or contaminated laminate. Properly prepare and scarf/taper the sound laminate around each hole to suit a structural repair schedule (often a broad taper around 12:1, subject to the laminate and repair design). Reinforce with successive fibreglass layers and epoxy; avoid a thick unsupported resin/filler plug. Allow full cure, inspect both sides, wash off any amine blush where relevant, sand and fair.

**Checkpoint:** no visible voids, incomplete wet-out, unsupported filler, soft laminate or pinholes before barrier coating. Photograph the layup and finished repair.

### 3. Cut and install new drains

Only drill after fixing the fitting type, outlet position, hose routing and heeled-waterline/valve decision. Dry-fit the fittings and hoses first. Maintain a clear fall where possible, avoid tight bends and chafe, and keep hose connections accessible. Seal all exposed laminate (and isolate any core) with epoxy and allow it to cure before bedding the fittings as specified by the product manufacturer. Follow the fitting maker's bedding, thread and torque instructions; don't overtighten composite parts.

**Checkpoint:** all connections accessible, hoses securely supported, no kinks, adequate drainage confirmed, and no backflow or leaks under relevant loading/heel conditions. Test the anchor well separately with water.

### 4. Sand, repair and roughly fair the hull

Remove loose antifoul and failed coatings using yard-approved methods. Avoid indiscriminately grinding away sound gelcoat. Feather old paint edges, repair local damage and roughly fair the repaired areas. The aim is a clean, sound, reasonably smooth substrate rather than a perfectly faired racing bottom.

### 5. Prepare keel

Identify whether the keel surface is iron/steel, lead or a coated composite before choosing abrasives, rust treatment and primer. Remove loose coating and corrosion, assess depth of pits and any movement or cracking at the hull/keel joint, then prime prepared bare metal promptly with the **manufacturer-approved system**. Fair pits only after the necessary substrate preparation/priming stages. Do not apply a generic hull barrier coat to unknown bare keel metal without checking compatibility.

**Checkpoint:** significant wastage, deep pitting or a suspicious keel joint requires further assessment, not just filler.

### 6. Prime underwater hull and keel

Wash/decontaminate and abrade as specified. Mask the waterline, then apply the specified number of coats to achieve the required film build. Log coat start time, air/substrate temperature, product, batch, thinning and quantity. Observe minimum and maximum overcoating intervals. If a tie coat is required for antifouling, apply it within the correct interval.

### 7. Paint topsides

Wash/degrease, remove failed paint, sand and feather edges. Repair small dents and scratches. Use primer/undercoat only where required by the selected topsides system. Mask carefully, use consistent roller technique and plan coats for suitable temperature, humidity and wind. Hot hull surfaces can shorten working and flow-out time; follow the paint datasheet. Protect the final finish from abrasion during the remaining jobs.

### 8. Antifoul

Apply the chosen antifouling at the specified coverage, thickness and number of coats. Give specified extra attention to the waterline, leading edges and rudder/keel where appropriate. Record final coat times and observe the product's launch window. Leave areas obscured by cradle pads to be completed after safe pad movement by the yard.

### 9. Final inspection and launch

Inspect all repair sites, coatings, edges and fittings. Photograph below-waterline repairs before launch. Verify drain operation and any valves or emergency plugs. Check all supports/pad areas are coated, confirm the antifoul and topsides have reached their required launch/handling state, and check after launching for any ingress or abnormal trim.

**Schedule contingencies:**

- Widespread or wet osmosis: stop opening new blisters; document and defer full treatment. If damage affects seaworthiness, arrange an alternative to relaunch rather than cover wet/unsound laminate.
- Structural surprises around through-hulls: complete proper laminate repairs before cosmetic work.
- Keel damage beyond surface pitting: investigate before painting over it.
- Drain design not approved: do not drill; resolve the design rather than install unsuitable fittings.
- Heat, humidity, wind or rain outside coating limits: defer that coat and reduce the paint scope, **not** the booked departure date.
- Primer/antifoul window missed: follow the manufacturer's specified recovery procedure; do not guess.
