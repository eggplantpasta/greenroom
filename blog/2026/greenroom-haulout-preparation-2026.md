Greenroom comes out at South of Perth Yacht Club on **Friday 11 December 2026 at 11:00 am**, and must leave the hardstand on **Wednesday 23 December**. This is the preparation checklist; the actual [hardstand work plan](../../greenroom-haulout-work-2026.md) tracks the jobs, daily decisions and progress once the boat is out.

**Boat:** 1976 Space Sailer 22, *Greenroom*  
**Status:** Preparing  
**Preparation day:** Friday 13 November 2026  
**Likely paint brand:** Jotun, to be confirmed on Friday  
**Approach:** Strip/sand back aged coatings as necessary, repair and repaint with rollers. Structural integrity and safe immersion take priority over finish.

> **Fixed departure:** 23 December cannot slip. Confirm product cure and immersion windows *before* stripping large areas. Work left incomplete may need a second lift in early January, but the boat must not be launched with open holes, unsafe laminate repairs or unsuitable/exposed underwater surfaces.

## Scope confirmed

- **Four old hull openings to close:** one **25 mm sink outlet** (not replaced), two **25 mm cockpit drains**, and one **10 mm anchor-well drain**. All have good access from both sides for grinding and fibreglassing.
- **Three new hull outlets:** two **25 mm TruDesign cockpit drains** through the hull sides, and one **25 mm TruDesign anchor-well drain**, positioned well above the waterline.
- The old cockpit drains exit underwater and have corroded bronze fittings. New drains are intended to have short, continuously falling hose runs through the aft locker, which is isolated from the cabin.
- **No seacocks planned** because the intended new outlets are above the applicable heeled waterline. Verify the *actual marked positions* and exact fitting installation instructions before drilling; do not rely solely on the cockpit floor's approximate 300 mm height above the upright waterline.
- Two **25 mm cockpit drains** are understood to comply with the boat's class and intended use; retain the applicable rule/reference in project notes and check actual clear internal bore, hose configuration and drainage in use.
- Underwater antifoul is over ten years old and substantially worn away. Topsides paint is approximately 50 years old. Both are to be sanded/prepared for a new coating system. Avoid grinding away sound gelcoat or intact protective laminate unnecessarily.
- Keel has reported pitting; final substrate and treatment to be confirmed on inspection. Any osmosis work is limited by realistic drying and the fixed launch date.

## Friday 13 November - dedicated preparation day

### 1. Paint supplier - main objective

- [ ] Visit the marine paint supplier and discuss **Jotun** systems (or a suitable alternative).
- [ ] Describe the boat: 22 ft fibreglass yacht; old worn antifoul; very old topsides paint; roller application; exposed keel/pitting; 13-day hardstand with only half-days available 15-17 December.
- [ ] Choose the **underwater fibreglass epoxy primer / barrier** system, including full coat count and specified film build.
- [ ] Confirm whether the **keel** needs a different metal primer, surface preparation or intermediate coat; do not assume hull primer is suitable for bare keel metal.
- [ ] Choose antifoul suitable for river/estuary sailing, occasional racing and planned conditions; check coverage, minimum/maximum launch intervals and desired finish.
- [ ] Choose topsides primer/undercoat and roller-applied finish, ideally **AS2700 G32 Opaline**, or record the best available tint.
- [ ] Confirm antifoul shade closest to **AS2700 B64 Charcoal**.
- [ ] Ask how to check remaining old one-pack/two-pack coatings for compatibility, and what removal is needed before applying the chosen system.
- [ ] Obtain technical data sheets (TDS) and safety data sheets (SDS) for **every** primer, finish, thinner, antifoul and epoxy product.
- [ ] Ask for the specified **substrate preparation**, abrasive grades, roller type, mixing ratios, pot life, thinning limits and application temperature range.
- [ ] Get **minimum and maximum recoat intervals**, including epoxy-to-antifoul tie-coat requirements, and **minimum immersion time** at realistic Perth temperatures.
- [ ] Work backwards from the **23 December launch**; identify the latest safe time for each coat, allowing for half-days 15-17 December.
- [ ] Get recommended litres per coat using the actual coating coverage (allowing for roller wastage and extra keel/waterline coats).
- [ ] Buy confirmed products or request a written quote; avoid buying a whole system until compatibility, timing and quantities check out.
- [ ] Record product names, codes, colours, pack sizes and prices below.

### 2. On the boat - measurements and photos

- [ ] Photograph the waterline, topsides, keel, rudder, fittings and obvious damage before the lift.
- [ ] Identify and label the **four** old connections: sink 25 mm, port cockpit 25 mm, starboard cockpit 25 mm, anchor well 10 mm.
- [ ] Measure fitting bodies, hose sizes and available inside/outside space; note whether the nominal sizes correspond to bore or fitting sizes.
- [ ] Check that inside access for taper grinding and glass layup remains clear after fittings are removed.
- [ ] Check hull laminate/core at the proposed new penetrations; identify any structure to avoid.
- [ ] Measure the existing cockpit drains, cockpit floor and both new outlet positions relative to the upright waterline; check likely heeled-waterline clearance.
- [ ] Check that hoses can fall continuously to the outlets without chafe, tight bends or inaccessible connections.
- [ ] Measure the anchor-well outlet route and verify the drain can sit above the well bottom's lowest water level while preserving fall.
- [ ] Check rear locker's seals, bulkhead and openings; consider the effect of a completely flooded locker on trim/stability.
- [ ] Measure approximate underwater hull, keel and topsides areas for paint quantity estimates.
- [ ] Check accessibility of rudder, keel underside and regions likely covered by cradle pads.
- [ ] **Do not drill new outlets while afloat.** Mark approximate positions for final confirmation after lift.

### 4. Tools, repairs and fittings - stocktake

- [ ] Check random-orbit sander, grinder and dust extraction (including appropriate filtration).
- [ ] Check abrasives: paint removal/preparation, feathering, long-board fairing and keel corrosion removal.
- [ ] Check access to a suitable ladder/platform and ask the yard about working near supports.
- [ ] Count laminating epoxy/hardener, fibreglass cloth, suitable structural filler and fairing filler for **four** hole repairs, with contingency.
- [ ] Check resin/hardener speed and workable pot life for Perth December temperatures; obtain the manufacturer's hole-repair laminate guidance.
- [ ] Make four hole-repair templates or a written layup schedule once actual opening diameters and laminate thickness are measured.
- [ ] Confirm exact **three 25 mm TruDesign** outlet models, connection type, hole saw sizes, bedding/sealant and permitted above-heeled-waterline installation.
- [ ] Buy the appropriate marine hose length and matching clamps; provide supports and chafe protection.
- [ ] Check that sealing exposed hole edges/core with epoxy can be completed and cured before fitting bedding/sealant.
- [ ] Stock mixing cups/pumps, stirrers, gloves, roller frames/covers, brushes, trays, tape, sheeting, solvent and labelled waste bags.
- [ ] Check respirator and correct cartridges/filters for dust and organic vapour as specified in the SDS, plus eye/hearing protection and coveralls.
- [ ] Have water, sun protection, first-aid supplies and a suitable fire extinguisher.
- [ ] Arrange an extra pair of hands for removals, larger sanding jobs and time-critical painting.
